Groundnut seed flow analysis in a rotating seed coater 
with discrete element method 
 
K Charith Kumar, G Rajender and M Padma 
 
DOI: https://www.doi.org/10.33545/2618060X.2026.v9.i5m.5785  
 
Abstract 
Coating of particulate solids by a thin film layer is of interest in many industrial applications such as seed 
and tablet coating. In seed processing, seeds are commonly coated with a protective coating layer consisting 
of fertilizers and disease control agents, such as pesticides and fungicides. Batch coaters are commonly used 
for this purpose. A typical coater consists of a horizontal axis cylindrical drum with a horizontal rotation and 
the coating material fed simultaneously after the seed. The seeds are driven around the drum in horizontal 
axis, and are mixed by three baffles; one on either side of the drum. In the present study, Distinct Element 
Method (DEM) simulations are used to model the seed coating process. Groundnut seed are used as a model 
material. The velocity of the seeds is predicted by the DEM, whereby the seeds are simulated as spheres 
inside a drum. It was observed that when the seed treatment drum was positioned at an angle of 45º, linear 
drum speed of 30 m/s the groundnut particles can attain a maximum velocity of 2.5 m/s. 
 
Keywords: Seed coating, discrete element method, simulation, etc 
 
Introduction  
Seed quality is one of the most important factors determining successful crop establishment, 
productivity, and overall agricultural sustainability. The maintenance of high-quality seed depends 
on several environmental factors, including moisture content, temperature, relative humidity, and 
storage conditions. However, even under controlled storage conditions, seed quality may 
deteriorate due to seed-borne diseases, storage insects, and other pest infestations. Among the 
various approaches adopted for protecting seeds, chemical seed treatment has been recognized as 
one of the most economical and effective methods for improving seed quality and ensuring better 
crop performance. Since most seed treatment chemicals are toxic in nature, proper application 
methods and safety precautions are essential during treatment and subsequent handling of treated 
seeds. 
Seed treatment is defined as the application of fungicides, insecticides, or combinations of 
chemicals to seeds to protect them from seed-borne and soil-borne pathogens and storage pests. It 
also includes various physical and biological approaches such as solar exposure and conditioning 
treatments. Treated seeds are subjected to specific processes intended to reduce, control, or repel 
disease-causing organisms, insects, and other pests that affect seed germination and early seedling 
growth. Pre-sowing seed treatments improve germination, enhance seedling vigour, prevent seed 
rot and seedling blight, protect seeds during storage, and provide resistance against soil and 
storage insects. 
The movement of granular materials inside rotating equipment is difficult to characterize 
experimentally because of the complex interactions among particles and the continuous dynamic 
changes occurring during operation. Measurement of individual particle velocities and flow 
patterns inside a rotating seed treatment drum is particularly challenging. Therefore, numerical 
modelling approaches have become valuable tools for predicting particle behaviour and 
optimizing equipment design. 
The Discrete Element Method (DEM) is one of the most widely used numerical techniques for 
analysing granular materials. DEM was initially developed by Cundall and Strack (1979) [3] for 
studying the behaviour of granular assemblies in rock mechanics and has subsequently been 
applied in various fields involving particle flow.

Unlike continuum-based approaches, DEM considers particles as 
discrete elements and calculates particle interactions by solving 
Newton’s laws of motion. The method incorporates particle size, 
shape, mass, rotational motion, and contact forces, making it 
suitable for investigating the dynamic behaviour of granular 
materials. 
DEM has been successfully applied in different processing 
industries to evaluate mixing, segregation, and coating processes. 
In coating systems, controlling inter-particle coating variability is 
critical because variations in coating thickness or mass can 
influence the effectiveness of applied chemicals and nutrients. 
Studies on rotating coating devices have demonstrated the 
usefulness of DEM in predicting particle circulation, mixing 
behaviour, and coating uniformity. Pandey et al. investigated 
particle motion in a pan coating device using experimental 
observations and DEM simulations and reported that dynamic 
angle of repose was influenced by rotational speed and fill level. 
Li et al. used DEM combined with spray analysis to investigate 
the effect of particle size distribution on segregation and coating 
uniformity in an industrial paddle coater. Similarly, Sandadi et al. 
developed imaging techniques to evaluate circulation time, 
surface exposure time, and particle exposure during coating 
processes. Groundnut (Arachis hypogaea L.) is an important 
oilseed crop cultivated worldwide and is a major source of edible 
oil, protein, minerals, and vitamins. It contains approximately 48–
50% oil and 26–28% protein and plays a significant role in 
agricultural economies, particularly in developing countries. 
India is one of the largest producers of groundnut, with major 
contributions from states such as Gujarat, Tamil Nadu, Andhra 
Pradesh, Karnataka, and Maharashtra. Due to its economic 
importance and nutritional value, maintaining high-quality 
groundnut seed through effective treatment and coating 
technologies is essential. 
Although seed treatment drums are widely used commercially, 
limited information is available regarding the particle flow 
behaviour and velocity distribution of groundnut seeds inside 
rotating seed treatment equipment. The complex interactions 
between seed particles and drum components make experimental 
determination of flow characteristics difficult. Hence, numerical 
simulation using DEM provides an effective approach for 
analysing seed movement, optimizing drum design, and 
improving coating uniformity. 
Therefore, the present study aims to calibrate the DEM 
parameters required for modelling groundnut seed behaviour and 
simulate particle movement inside a seed treatment drum to 
determine the velocity profile of groundnut particles. The specific 
objectives of the study are to calibrate DEM properties required 
for modelling groundnut, including physical and interaction 
properties and mixing behaviour. The study also about 
simulateing particle movement inside a seed treatment drum and 
determine the velocity profile of groundnut particles. 
 
Materials and Methods 
Raw Materials 
The ground seed for experiment was procured from Sangareddy, 
Medak. The seed was cleaned and stored in plastic containers for 
the use. 
 
Size and shape 
The size of the black pepper was measured with a dial type 
thickness gauge having accuracy of ± 0.01mm. 100 seeds were 
randomly selected and measurements were taken at three different 
orientations.  
 
Seed drum description: Seed treatment drum is made of angle 
iron frame and G. I. sheet made drum. Bearings provided with this 
implement make it easy to use. In one batch 10-15kg of seed can 
be treated with chemicals. The drum is provided with four number 
of G. I. sheet made fins placed all along the length at an angle of 
900 from each other. This special arrangement of fins makes this 
seed treatment drum very efficient. The drum and frame of this 
equipment are strong and durable. Seed treatment drum is the 
liking of the farmers. 
The seed treatment drum consists of frame, handle and cylindrical 
drum. The cylindrical drum is mounted on a tri-pod angle iron 
frame. Three piece of mild steel flat are welded inside the drum 
for helping in uniform mixing. Prior to star mixing of chemicals, 
workers are advised for wearing plastic hand gloves and mask on 
nose for health protection. After Adding chemicals in drum, add 
little water, close the lid of drum tightly and rotate the drum for 
20 to 25 revolutions. After 1-2 minutes of completing the work, 
open the lid and take the treated seed in a separate bag/container. 
A batch of 20kg seeds takes about 5-6 minute for complete 
operation.

Simulation Methodology: It is essential to know the accurate set 
of DEM parameters to model the bulk behavior of the material 
(Coetzee, 2016) [2]. DEM input parameters can be categorized as 
material properties and interaction properties. Size, shape, 
particle density, Young's modulus, shear modulus and Poission’s 
ratio constitute material properties, whereas, interaction 
properties include the coefficient of restitution, static friction, 
rolling friction between particle to particle contact and particle to 
surface contact respectively (Boac et al., 2010) [1]. EDEM 2.4.1 
Academic Version from DEM Solutions was used in this study. 
The DEM parameters for the work were found from Generic 
EDEM Material Model (GEMM) Database. The GEMM database 
contains thousands of material models representing a wide range 
of materials such as rocks, soils and ores. EDEM software is made 
of three core components: EDEM Creator, Simulator and Analyst.

Bulk density (ρb) 
The bulk density was determined by filling a container of a known 
volume with a sample of the bulk material. The bulk mass, m, of 
the material was used together with the container volume, to 
calculate the bulk density,  
 
ρb = m / Vb. 
 
Angle of repose 
The angle of repose was also used experimentally and 
numerically to see which method was more accurate in this case. 
The angle of repose or critical angle of repose, of a granular 
material is the steepest angle of descent or dip relative to the 
horizontal plane to which a material can be piled without 
slumping. At this angle, the material on the slope face is on the 
verge of sliding. The angle of repose can range from 0° to 90°. 
The angle of repose was measured by filling a bottomless tapered 
cylindrical container with material. The container had a lower 
diameter of 200mman upper diameter of 220mmwith a height of 
500mm. The container was then slowly lifted vertically to allow 
the material to exit at the bottom and forma static pile.Angle of 
repose is the steepest angle at which a sloping surface formed of 
loose material is stable. The height of the pile is measured from 
base to top.  
 
The equation for calculating the angle of repose is: 
 
Angle of repose = Tan-1(2h/d). 
 
Where, h is height of the pile and d is diameter of the pile

Velocity of the groundnut particles simulated with DEM 
The drum was given a rotational motion in the DEM and velocity 
profiles formed during the simulation was studied. The velocity 
of the simulated particles varied between 0.2 and 2.5 m/s. The 
results showed that initially the velocity increased constantly until 
0.23 s and stabilized afterwards with minor fluctuations as shown 
in the Fig. 4.2. Average velocities at different time steps showed 
a steady increase with time. However, it should be observed that 
the average velocity of the particles is much lesser than the 
maximum velocities which are in the order of 0.5 to 0.9 m/s only. 
It can be observed that, the minimum velocities of the particles 
are less than 0.25 m/s only. The range of minimum velocities 
indicate that during the simulation there are always some particles 
which might be having minimal movement. The simulations 
results showed an understanding of particles velocities in a 
rotating drum. The velocity profiles can be used as guide to set 
the rpm of the rotating drum.

4. Summary and Conclusions: Seed treatment is an important 
process in the seed industry to improve seed germination and 
vigour. The performance of a seed treatment drum (seed coater) 
depends on its design and operating parameters, which must be 
optimized according to seed properties to avoid seed damage and 
ensure uniform coating. Conventional experimental methods have 
limitations in measuring important parameters such as particle 
velocity and impact forces inside the drum. 
The Discrete Element Method (DEM) provides an effective 
numerical approach for analysing granular particle behaviour in 
seed coaters. In DEM simulations, seed particles are represented 
by discrete elements, and their movement characteristics such as 
maximum, minimum, and average velocities can be predicted at 
different operating conditions. The study of groundnut seed 
movement using DEM showed that drum angle and rotational 
speed significantly influence particle velocity. At a drum 
inclination of 45° and a linear drum speed of 30 m s⁻¹, groundnut 
particles attained a maximum velocity of 2.5 m s⁻¹, demonstrating 
the usefulness of DEM in optimizing seed treatment drum 
performance.
